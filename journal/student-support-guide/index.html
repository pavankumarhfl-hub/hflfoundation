Student Support That Works: A Practical Guide for Schools and Communities

A practical guide for schools and communities to identify learning barriers, support students and measure progress responsibly.

← Back to the HFL Foundation Journal

Student opportunity is broader than scholarships alone. Students may need mentoring, access to learning materials, practical projects, safe spaces to ask questions, career guidance or a connection to a school or community programme. A useful support system begins by understanding the need rather than assuming every learner needs the same intervention.

Start with listening

Talk with students, educators, families and community partners. Record the problem in clear language, protect personal information and avoid collecting data that is not needed. If a programme serves children, safeguarding and appropriate adult oversight must be part of the design from the beginning.

Make support practical

  • Identify the specific barrier: learning resources, transport, digital access, confidence, mentoring or information.
  • Set a small, measurable goal that can be reviewed within a realistic period.
  • Match students with suitable resources or trained volunteers rather than promising outcomes that cannot be guaranteed.
  • Provide an accessible route for students to ask questions or report concerns.

Measure what changed

Count participation, but do not stop there. Where appropriate, track attendance, completion, learner feedback, follow-up needs and whether support reached the intended group. Explain how figures are defined, what period they cover and what limitations remain. Do not publish identifiable student stories or photographs without appropriate informed consent.

How to contribute

Educators can propose a school partnership, students can volunteer skills within their experience, and organisations can offer learning materials or structured mentoring. Start with a clear proposal: who it serves, what is needed, who will supervise it and how results will be reviewed.
